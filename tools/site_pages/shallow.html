When the bottom shows through

Over shallow water, the light that comes back up is partly the water's and partly the bottom's. How much of each depends on how deep the bottom is, how bright it is, and how clear the water is. This page puts a flat bottom under each of the ten Jerlov types and evaluates Lee et al.'s published model of what a sensor looking straight down would see.

The model

A fit to radiative transfer

Lee, Carder, Mobley, Steward and Patch fitted a model of the reflectance just beneath the surface to HydroLight runs: a uniform water with Petzold's average-particle phase function over a flat, Lambertian bottom (Appl. Opt. 37, 6329, 1998), and refitted it with more runs a year later, correcting a calculation error (Appl. Opt. 38, 3831, 1999). jerlov uses only the 1999 constants, in lee1999_shallow_rrs.

The water column and the bottom each contribute a term. The column's grows with depth towards the deep-water value; the bottom's fades twice over, on the way down and on the way back, by the depth times a + bb.

rrs  = rrs_dp (1 − exp(−(1/cos θw + 1.03 (1 + 2.4 u)^0.5) κ H))
     + (ρ/π) exp(−(1/cos θw + 1.04 (1 + 5.4 u)^0.5) κ H)
rrs_dp = (0.084 + 0.170 u) u,   u = bb/(a + bb),   κ = a + bb
Rrs  = 0.5 rrs / (1 − 1.5 rrs)

H is the bottom depth, ρ its albedo, θw the sun's angle in the water (refracted with n = 1.34, the paper's), rrs_dp the reflectance of the same water with no bottom, and Rrs the reflectance just above the surface.

How far to trust it

Against a Monte Carlo

jerlov set the model against its own Monte Carlo, {{MC_RUNS}} runs with another of Petzold's phase functions and a flat surface. The median of the model over the Monte Carlo is {{MC_MEDIAN}}, and it is within 10 percent in {{MC_WITHIN}} percent of the runs. It misses in two groups: it runs low where pure water carries most of the backscattering, and high over a dark bottom a few metres down in turbid water. Both are drawn in question 5 of “Where the sources disagree”.

What the model leaves out: views other than straight down (the 1999 paper's change for them is not used), a sky that is not the sun's beam, Raman scattering and fluorescence, a bottom that is not flat or not Lambertian, and water that is not uniform. The controls stop at the runs' own limits: the bottom from 0.5 to 32 m, the sun at the runs' three angles, 400 to 700 nm.

Every value on this page comes from jerlov ; the page evaluates the equations above on them, as lee1999_shallow_rrs does. The water's a is Solonenko & Mobley's (2015), the only source with a and b for all ten types; they give it every 25 nm, and between those wavelengths the values are the package's linear interpolation. bb is Water.bb with the particles' bbp/bp chosen above: 0.0183 is that of Petzold's average-particle phase function, which Lee et al.'s runs used, and 0.005 and 0.03 bracket it. Neither the albedo nor bbp/bp is set by the Jerlov type, so both are yours to choose; the package ships no albedos of sea beds. The colours are π Rrs under Gregg & Carder's (1990) clear sky above the surface (the sky of “Daylight on the way down”: 21 June, a visibility of {{VISIBILITY}} km, at the chosen sun), through the CIE 1931 observer from 400 to 700 nm with that sky as white, clipped to sRGB. The sky reflected by the surface is not in Rrs and not in the colours. DECISIONS.md section 121 says how the page was made, DATA.md section 50 where the model's numbers came from.
